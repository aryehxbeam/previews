It's a Vegetable. Stop Putting It In Pies.

(We know you won't stop. We're not going to stop either.)

Botanically, rhubarb is family Polygonaceae, genus Rheum, and the part you eat is the petiole - the leaf stalk. That's a vegetable by every definition that matters. And yet humanity has collectively decided it belongs in crumbles, jams, and pies alongside actual fruit. In 19th-century America it was literally nicknamed the pie plant.

Modern cultivated rhubarb is a tetraploid with 2n = 44 chromosomes, double the 2n = 22 of its wild ancestors. Evolution looked at a sour stalk and said: more.

The Identity Crisis, In Code

from enum import Enum

class Reality(Enum):
    VEGETABLE = "petiole (leaf stalk)"
    FRUIT = "what everyone insists on treating it as"

def classify(plant: str) -> Reality:
    """The single most contested function in botanical computing."""
    if plant == "rhubarb":
        legal_ruling = Reality.FRUIT   # NY court, 1947, for tariff reasons
        botany       = Reality.VEGETABLE
        return botany  # ...but the pie lobby disagrees
    raise ValueError(f"{plant} is not nearly as confused as rhubarb")

assert classify("rhubarb") is Reality.VEGETABLE  # cope

Fun fact: a 1947 New York court really did rule rhubarb a fruit, because it was cheaper to import that way. Capitalism: 1, Botany: 0.

The Leaves Will Absolutely Kill You toxic

The stalk is a dessert. The leaf, six inches away, is a nephrotoxin.

Rhubarb leaves contain oxalic acid (a nephrotoxin) plus some additional toxins science hasn't fully fingered yet. The same plant offers you a delightful stalk and a leaf that attacks your kidneys. Nature has a sense of humor and it is dark.

Governments Have Made This Mistake. Twice.

During World War I, the British government printed instructions recommending rhubarb leaves as a food source. People died. During World War II, the same instructions resurfaced - "leading to further deaths." A bug shipped to production, rolled back, and then re-deployed 25 years later. Relatable. 

// A type system that refuses to let you eat the wrong part.

fn is_edible(part: RhubarbPart) -> Result<&'static str, &'static str> {

    // This compiles. Eating the Err variant does not.

}

Worth More Than Saffron

For centuries rhubarb was a luxury import along the Silk Road. The root, dried, was the prize - prized as a medicine, traded like treasure.

It shows up 1,800 years ago in The Divine Farmer's Herb-Root Classic. By the 10th century Rheum ribes was eaten across the Islamic world. By the 14th it had reached Europe through Aleppo and Smyrna as "Turkish rhubarb." Marco Polo went hunting for it in the mountains of Tangut province, where it reportedly cost more than cinnamon, opium, and saffron combined.

SELECT good,

The Rhubarb Triangle 🔺

There is a region of Yorkshire where rhubarb is grown in total darkness and harvested by candlelight. This is real. This is a job people have.

"Forced rhubarb" is grown in heated sheds with all other light excluded, then harvested by candle so stray daylight doesn't spoil it. The result is a sweeter, more tender pink stalk. The practice is concentrated in a triangle between three towns: Wakefield, Leeds, and Morley - the legendary Rhubarb Triangle. You can supposedly hear the stalks creak as they grow in the dark.

Forcing, Modeled In Clojure

(def rhubarb-triangle #{:wakefield :leeds :morley})

(defn force-rhubarb
  "Grow rhubarb in the dark. Returns sweeter, pinker, creakier stalks."
  [root]
  (-> root
      (assoc :light :absolutely-none
             :harvested-by :candlelight)
      (update :sweetness * 1.4)
      (update :tenderness * 1.6)
      (assoc :sound-effect "🎻 faint creaking")))

(->> (repeatedly 3 #(force-rhubarb {:sweetness 5 :tenderness 5}))
     (map :sound-effect))

Assorted Rhubarb Lore

🍓

The Unhappy Marriage

Strawberry-rhubarb is a classic pairing, but some "rhubarb purists jokingly consider this a rather unhappy marriage." Divorce proceedings ongoing since approximately 1860.

🇫🇮

Finnish Mead

Finland makes sima, a mead variation, with rhubarb. Because if your plant can be a fruit, a vegetable, AND a poison, it can certainly also be an alcohol.

🇺🇸

Jefferson's Spinach Error

Thomas Jefferson planted R. undulatum at Monticello in 1809, noting the leaves were "excellent as Spinach." The leaves which, again, are poisonous. Founding Father, questionable forager.

🌱

Earlier Than You Think

John Bartram grew rhubarb in Philadelphia in the 1730s - decades before the usual "arrived in the 1820s" story. The timeline, like the plant, is contested.

"Esculent rhubarb, the leaves excellent as Spinach." - Thomas Jefferson, 1809, describing a plant whose leaves would have poisoned him
